# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be respectful. Assume good faith. Discuss the work, not the person.

This project exists to make online spaces less hostile. It would be absurd to be hostile while building
it.

## Working with offensive language

PersianTextGuard is a profanity filter, so slurs, sexual insults and harassment terms appear in the word
lists, the tests, the conformance corpus and in bug reports. Quoting them, categorising them and arguing
about whether a word belongs in `slur` or `insult` is the work, and nobody should feel awkward doing it
carefully.

What separates that from abuse is simple:

- **Clinical, with a purpose**: "«کسکش» should be `insult`, not `profanity`, because…", or pasting the
  exact message that slipped through.
- **Not acceptable**: aiming any of those words at a person, quoting them for effect, or sending far more
  offensive material than the report needs.

When you report a miss or a false positive, send the offending text and nothing more: no usernames, no
screenshots of private conversations, no personal data of the people involved. Issues are public and
permanent.

## Expected behaviour

- Be welcoming to newcomers, and to people whose first language is Persian, English or neither. Imperfect
  English in an issue is not a defect; the content is what matters.
- Accept that a maintainer may say no. This project rejects changes that raise false positives even when
  they catch more evasions — that is a design decision, not a dismissal of your idea.
- Give feedback on the change, not the author. "This would flag «هر کس»" is review; "this is careless" is
  not.
- Respect people's time: search before opening a duplicate, include what a reader needs, and follow up on
  your own pull requests.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, whether public or private: insults, threats, stalking, unwanted contact, or sustained
  disruption of discussions.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments about ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender, sexual
  orientation, disability, age or appearance — including "as a joke", and including in Persian.
- Sexual attention or imagery directed at anyone in the project's spaces.
- Publishing someone's private information, or anyone else's messages, without their consent.
- Deliberately using the project's own subject matter as cover for abuse: quoting slurs *at* a
  participant, or filing "test" issues full of offensive text that serve no report.

## Where this applies

Issues, pull requests, code review, commit messages, discussions, and any space where you represent the
project — including speaking as a contributor elsewhere.

## Reporting

If someone behaves badly, tell the maintainer privately instead of replying in kind:

- Open a private report through the repository's
  [Security tab](https://github.com/AmirehsanK/PersianTextGuard/security/advisories/new) — it is the
  private channel this repository has, and it is fine to use it for conduct as well. Say up front that
  the report is about conduct, not a vulnerability.
- Or email the maintainer directly: **amirehsank3@gmail.com**. Put "PersianTextGuard conduct" in the
  subject so it is not lost.
- Or contact the maintainer, [@AmirehsanK](https://github.com/AmirehsanK), through GitHub.
- For behaviour that breaks GitHub's own rules, you can also use GitHub's
  [report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) form, which reaches GitHub staff directly.

Reports are read by the maintainer. Your report and your identity are kept private; details are shared
only if you agree, or where GitHub's own processes require it. Reporting something in good faith will
never count against you, even if the outcome is that nothing was wrong.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides what is appropriate, in proportion to what happened and whether it is repeated.
Typically, in order:

1. **A private word** explaining what was wrong and why, sometimes with a request to edit or withdraw a
   comment.
2. **A public correction** on the thread, when the behaviour was public and left a false impression.
3. **A temporary block** from the project's spaces, with the reason stated.
4. **A permanent block**, for sustained harassment, threats, or a clear pattern after warnings.

Content that violates this code — abusive comments, doxxing, "test" issues of gratuitous offensive text —
may be edited, locked or deleted, with a note that it was.

## Scope of this document

This is a small project, maintained by one person. This document exists so that expectations are written
down rather than assumed, not to imply a committee. It is adapted for a project whose subject matter is
offensive language, and is informed by the community guidelines common to open-source projects.
